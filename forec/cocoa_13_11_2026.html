Analytical Forecast: The market has changed its driver. On Nov 11 Ivory Coast's state weather agency Sodexam warned that a "Super El Niño 2026-2027" could cut the 2026/27 cocoa crop by 21–25%, with annual rainfall down 10–27%; an Abidjan export director called arrivals "below all of our initial forecasts" (Reuters via TimesLIVE, Nov 11). March cocoa answered with two strong sessions, +3.6% and +3.1%, from $4,871 to $5,204 on rising volume (29.0K and 26.2K contracts). Our primary scenario (45%) is a continuation of short covering into the lower edge of the 3-month volume bank at $5,388 and on into the $5,390–$5,620 target zone within 5–10 sessions; ADMIS expects West African soils to dry out over the next 1–1.5 weeks, which keeps the weather premium alive (Vibiz, Nov 12). A flat scenario (30%) has price stalling under $5,388 as rising ICE stocks absorb the weather bid. The bearish scenario (25%) needs a close below $5,000, which would show the rebound was only a technical bounce inside the downtrend.

External Analysts: Vibiz Research (Nov 12) expected consolidation with resistance at $5,011–$5,115 on the December contract; price is already above that band. ADMIS points to drying West African soils (bullish). No fresh numeric targets with a horizon under two months were found.

2. Why (Fundamental & Microstructure Breakdown)

2.1 Fundamental Drivers (Primary Foundation): Supply shock risk: a 21–25% cut would remove roughly a quarter of the world's largest origin; Ivory Coast harvested over 2 million tonnes last season and had already sold about 1.15 million tonnes of the 2026/27 main crop forward before halting sales in June (Reuters via TimesLIVE). Forward sales against a shrinking crop raise the risk of export shortfalls. Counterweight: ICE certified stocks keep climbing to 736,768 bags (+4.2% in one day, +10.7% over four reports), bags passing grading jumped +10.1% to 327,894, and total warehouse holdings hit a new high of 3,763,466 bags. Visible US supply is ample today; the market is pricing next year's crop, and stock growth caps the speed of the rally. Nigeria's September cocoa exports rose 33% y/y. Major commodity indices announced lower cocoa weightings for 2027, a January-rebalance headwind rather than a factor for this window.
